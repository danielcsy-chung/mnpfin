01 · Access and accounts

One token, one browser, one account — forever

Access is invite-only. A token is a single-use key: the first browser that redeems it is permanently bound to it, and the token will not open on any second browser. That binding lives on the server, not in your cache, so clearing your cache does not free the token up again.

What clearing your cache does do is wipe your local copy of everything. That is why creating an account is mandatory. The account is the portable half of the system: user ID and password are stored server-side, so you can log in from a school laptop, a phone, a friend's machine, anywhere, and your entire history comes down with you.

How to use it well Log in rather than re-redeeming. If you ever get "this token has already been redeemed on another browser", that is the system working correctly — switch to the login form with the link under the button and use your account instead.
If you forget your password there is no self-service reset. Ask the administrator (contact details are on the login screen) to reset it for you.

02 · Dashboard

The one screen that tells you whether to be worried

  • D-n days. The big number is a plain countdown to your exam date. No ring, no percentage, nothing to interpret — D-42 means forty-two days left. After the date it flips to D+n in red.
  • Bank bar. How much of the question bank you have touched at least once.
  • Pills. Attempted count, sessions run, size of the Redemption queue, overall accuracy.
  • Where you stand. Three cards: your accuracy windows, an L10 sparkline by session, and your three weakest types by L50 with a one-click button to go grind the worst one.
  • Resume bar. If a paused session exists it appears above everything else in brass. It does not expire.
How to use it well Look at one thing: is L10 above or below OVR? Above means the recent work is landing. Below means you are getting worse, or you have moved into harder material — check the per-type table in Analytics before you panic.

03 · Practice modes

Five ways in, each for a different job

  • Timed. Clock runs, no feedback until the end, pacing logged per question type. Anything over twice the 71-second target is auto-flagged even when you got it right. This is the only mode that produces pace data.
  • Untimed. Feedback the instant you submit: correct answer, full rationale, why each distractor fails, and a row of error-reason buttons. This is where actual learning happens.
  • Open book. Untimed plus a third panel showing your own Toolbox notes filtered to the current question type. Use it when you know a rule exists but cannot recall it under pressure.
  • Mock Test. Two modules, 27 questions each, 32 minutes, question-type ratios matched to the real thing. Drawn from unseen questions only.
  • Category / Flagged / Targeted. Narrow slices — one or more of the 13 types, everything you flagged, or your Redemption queue, your collections, and unseen questions of the same type-and-difficulty signature as your misses.
How to use it well Untimed to learn a type, Timed to prove you can do it at speed, Mock to prove you can do it for 32 minutes straight. Doing only Timed teaches you nothing; doing only Untimed leaves you slow.

04 · Quick Practice

The on-the-go mode

Start it from the button inside the white welcome box on the Dashboard. No clock, no configuration, no fixed length — it hands you fresh questions with immediate feedback and keeps going. A red Finish button sits in the footer the whole time.

Finish is exclusive to Quick Practice. Every other session type runs until you complete it or exit it normally, precisely so a real session cannot be quietly abandoned halfway and still counted.

How to use it well Six questions in a queue, twelve on a bus. Because everything is logged the same way as any other session, five scattered Quick Practices in a day feed the same L10 window as one sit-down set — the analytics do not care where you were.

05 · Pause and resume

Stop anywhere, pick it up anywhere

Every session type — timed, untimed, open book, mock, quick — has a Pause button in the top bar. Pausing freezes the clock, saves the queue, your selections, your strikeouts, your flags and your elapsed time, and syncs the lot to your account.

Resume from the brass bar on the Dashboard. Because the paused state lives on the server, you can pause on a laptop and resume on a phone. In Timed mode the clock resumes from exactly where it stopped, so pausing gains you nothing on pace.

Only one paused session is held at a time. Starting a fresh session while one is paused will ask you to confirm before discarding it.

06 · The question interface

Top bar, left to right

  • Exit — leaves the session. Answered questions are already saved; the rest are dropped.
  • Pause — see above.
  • Flag (F) — marks the question for the Flagged queue, right or wrong.
  • Save (S) — drops the question into any collection.
  • Word (W) — logs a word you do not know. Select it in the passage first and it prefills.
  • Note (N) — your private note on this exact question. A brass dot on the button means a note already exists.
  • Notes — toggles the Open book panel.

Keyboard: A–D or 1–4 to select, Alt+letter to strike out, Enter to submit then advance, Esc to exit.

07 · Answer elimination

The same crossing-out tool as the real Bluebook app

Hit the ABC toggle above the choices. Each option grows a circular strike button on its right; click it to cross the option out, and the button becomes Undo. Crossed options stay struck when you navigate away and come back, and they are cleared for you on a new question. Alt + the letter does the same thing without touching the toggle.

How to use it well On Transitions and Rhetorical synthesis, cross out before you re-read the passage — it forces you to commit to a reason for rejecting each option instead of drifting toward whichever one feels nice. If you cannot articulate why an option is dead, do not cross it out.

08 · Word logging

Capture now, define later

Mid-question, highlight the word you did not know and press Word in the top right (or W). It is logged instantly with the question ID attached and you carry on — no interruption, no definition required in the moment.

Every logged word lands in the brass Word queue at the top of Toolbox → Vocabs. Click Define on any of them to fill in the part of speech, the common definition, the sense the test actually used, and your own note. Once defined it joins the main vocab grid.

How to use it well Empty the queue once a day, not once a week. Write the tested sense in the second field — Words-in-Context questions almost never turn on the dictionary-first meaning, and that second field is the one worth re-reading.

09 · Per-question notes

Your own annotation on every single question

Press Note during practice, or open any question from History, the Error log, or a set and use the note box in the detail view. Write whatever helps: the trap, the grammar rule, why you fell for C, the pattern this question shares with three others.

Notes are stored per question ID against your account, so they follow you across browsers and reappear the moment you meet that question again — including inside the feedback drawer when you get it wrong. Toolbox → Question notes lists every one, and lets you jump straight to a question by ID.

How to use it well Write the note in the form of the decision you should have made, not a description of the mistake. "Missed it, careless" is worthless. "Two independent clauses — semicolon or period, never a comma, even when the second one is short" is a rule you can apply next Tuesday.

10 · Sets and collections

Curated queues you build yourself

Redemption is built in and fills itself with every question you last got wrong. Beyond that you make your own — press Save mid-question, or use + New collection.

You can also add questions after the fact: the add-by-ID row at the top of the Sets page takes any question ID, from anywhere — a session you finished last week, the error log, a set you were reviewing — picks the target collection, and drops it in. It behaves identically to a question saved live: it shows up in the collection, counts toward the collection's progress bar, and is drawn on when you practise it.

Each collection shows how many of its questions you have attempted and your accuracy on them. Practice runs the whole collection; View opens the table, where every row has a note button and a remove button.

How to use it well Keep collections narrow and disposable. "Comma splices I fell for" beats "Grammar". When a narrow collection hits 90%, delete it — that is the point of it.

11 · Analytics

What the windows literally mean

  • L10 is the last 10 questions you attempted. Not the last session, not the last ten of some category — the ten most recent attempts, full stop. Attempt an eleventh and the oldest one drops out of the window and the newest enters it. The frame shifts forward one question at a time.
  • L20, L40 and L50 work identically but stay locked until you have actually attempted 20, 40 and 50 questions. A greyed-out window means not enough data, not 0%.
  • OVR is every attempt you have ever made.

Above the overall chart sits the per-type L10 trend: one line per question type, each showing that type's own rolling last-10 accuracy plotted against how many of that type you have done. Toggle types on and off with the chips — "Weakest five" is usually the only view you need.

Below: the per-type table with all four windows and an L10-vs-OVR arrow, your seconds-per-question by type from Timed mode against the 71-second target, and the auto-flagged over-pace list.

How to use it well Read L10 against OVR, never on its own — ten questions is a small enough window that one bad passage moves it 20 points. The per-type trend lines are where real improvement shows up: a line that climbs and then flattens above 80% is a type you can stop drilling.
Slow and correct is still a leak. The pace panel flags anything over 2× target regardless of outcome, because on the real test that time was borrowed from a later question.

12 · Toolbox

Five drawers

  • Personal notes — rules and heuristics tagged General or by question type. Open book mode reads from here.
  • Question notes — your per-question annotations, all in one list.
  • Vocabs — the pending word queue on top, your defined words below.
  • Transition bank — reserved for your own list, grouped by relationship.
  • Error logs / Flagged logs — every question you have ever missed, and everything you flagged. Nothing rolls off either one.
How to use it well Tag notes by type rather than General wherever you can. A General note shows up on every question in Open book; a type-tagged one shows up exactly when it is relevant, which is what makes the panel readable instead of a wall.

13 · History

Everything, question by question

Every session you have attempted, newest first. Open one and you get a row per question: ID, type, difficulty, your pick, the correct answer, time taken, the error reason you tagged, and whether you flagged it. View opens the full question with passage, choices, explanation, and your note.

How to use it well Same-day review is worth roughly double next-week review. Open the session you just finished, view every question you missed, and write the note while you can still remember what you were thinking when you picked the wrong one.

14 · Putting it together

A routine that uses all of this

  1. Daily, 15 minutes. Quick Practice on the go. Log words as you meet them, flag anything that felt shaky, Finish whenever you run out of time.
  2. Daily, 10 minutes. Empty the word queue in Toolbox → Vocabs. Write per-question notes on everything you missed today, straight from History.
  3. Three times a week. Open Analytics, look at the per-type trend, pick the weakest type, run 20 questions of it Untimed. Build a narrow collection from what you miss.
  4. Twice a week. Timed set of 22 to keep pace honest. Check the seconds-per-type panel afterwards, not just the score.
  5. Weekly. Grind Redemption. A question you get right on the second pass leaves the queue; one you miss again is the real problem and deserves a note.
  6. Every two weeks. Full Mock Test, both modules, no pausing. This is the only thing that tests stamina.
The single highest-leverage habit Write the per-question note the same day you miss the question. Everything else on this site is measurement; that note is the only part that changes what you will do next time.
